# its empty

**its empty** is a Skill from **its by oragment**.

It is an upstream Skill for **its shape**.

It does not form Structure.

It does not generate Idea or Work.

It only addresses one state:

> The user has almost no fragments that can be placed side by side, and does not know where to begin.

Its only task:

> Help the user produce a first set of usable fragments, then hand those fragments to **its shape**.

---

## 1. Purpose

Use **its empty** when the user feels:

- blank
- empty
- unable to start
- without notes, thoughts, or scraps to work from
- as if there is “nothing to put down”

Do not use **its empty** when the user already has enough material to begin Connect.

In that case, use **its shape**.

If the user has too much material, that belongs to **its chaos** (when available).

If the user has material but feels stuck inside a narrow line of thought, that belongs to **its tight** (when available).

---

## 2. Success criteria

The default goal is to collect **3–5 usable fragments**.

This is not a rigid quota:

- if **3** fragments are already enough to begin Connect, stop
- if more than **5** appear naturally, do not cut them down just to hit a number
- if the user offers a strong fragment early, keep it; do not discard it to fit a template

Success is **not**:

- finding a theme
- finding a direction
- finding a Mainline
- producing an Observation, Question, Idea, or Work

Success **is**:

> There is enough material to begin **its shape** at Connect.

As soon as that state is reached, hand off immediately.

Do not continue shaping.

---

## 3. Core principles

### 3.1 Upstream only

its empty ends when fragments are ready for Connect.

It never enters Structure.

### 3.2 Notice before meaning

Help the user notice concrete things.

Do not explain what those things “really mean.”

### 3.3 Collect, do not conclude

Fragments are source material.

Do not summarize them into a theme while collecting.

### 3.4 Few openings at a time

Do not turn Notice into a questionnaire.

Offer a small number of concrete perceptual openings — usually one, at most two or three.

Do not dump a long list of prompts.

### 3.5 Preserve the user’s words

When the user writes or speaks a fragment, keep it close to their wording.

Do not silently rewrite it into a cleaner idea.

### 3.6 One stage at a time

Move gradually:

**Empty → Open → Notice → Collect → Fragments → Hand off**

Do not skip ahead to shaping stages from **its shape**.

### 3.7 Hand off cleanly

When enough fragments exist, say so clearly and stop.

Invite the user to continue with **its shape**.

Do not begin Connect, Mainline, Observation, Question, Idea, or Work inside this Skill unless the environment explicitly switches Skills and the user asks to continue there.

---

## 4. Workflow

# Stage 1 — Empty

## Goal

Acknowledge that there is not enough material yet.

## Actions

Recognize the blank state without diagnosing it.

Do not rush to explain why the user has “no ideas.”

Do not comfort with motivational talk.

A simple recognition is enough, then move toward Open.

Example tone:

> There may not be much to work with yet. We can start by noticing something small.

---

# Stage 2 — Open

## Goal

Open attention.

Move from blankness into a state where something can be perceived.

## Actions

Shift attention from “finding an idea” toward noticing something concrete.

Openings may involve:

- the body
- the immediate surroundings
- a recent moment
- a faint pull of curiosity or discomfort

Open is not theme generation.

Avoid:

> What project do you want to create?
> What is your main goal?

Prefer:

> Before looking for an idea, notice what is already nearby.

Keep this brief. Then move to Notice.

---

# Stage 3 — Notice

## Goal

Point attention toward something concrete.

## Good Notice targets

- a small recent moment that made them pause
- a thought that keeps returning
- a detail they saw but have not explained
- a slight discomfort, doubt, or curiosity
- a recent person, object, place, or change
- something unfinished that keeps appearing at the edge of attention

## Notice principles

Notice should be specific.

Avoid abstract topic prompts such as:

> What are your values?
> What industry are you interested in?
> What content do you want to make?

Prefer concrete invitations such as:

> What is one small thing that made you pause recently?
> Is there a detail you keep noticing without explaining?
> What thought returned more than once this week?

## How many openings

Default: offer **one** Notice opening.

If the user cannot respond, offer a different concrete opening.

Do not present a long menu of prompts at once.

Do not interview the user with many questions in a row.

---

# Stage 4 — Collect

## Goal

Turn what was noticed into short fragments.

## Fragment qualities

A usable fragment should be:

- short
- independent
- close to the original feeling
- able to sit beside other fragments later
- not yet summarized
- not yet categorized

## Collect actions

Help the user write the noticed thing down as a fragment.

If the user speaks in a long paragraph, help them extract one short fragment — with their wording as much as possible.

If useful, mirror back a candidate fragment and ask whether it feels accurate.

Example:

User:

> I keep thinking about the old notebook on my desk that I never open.

Candidate fragment:

> The old notebook on my desk that I never open.

Then ask if they want to keep it as written, or adjust it.

## Keep collecting lightly

After one fragment is collected, return to Notice for another concrete opening, or deepen the same line with a nearby detail.

Do not start relating fragments to one another yet.

Do not say what they are “about.”

---

# Stage 5 — Fragments

## Goal

Hold the growing set of fragments as a simple list.

## Actions

Keep the collected fragments visible and numbered or clearly separated.

Example:

1. The old notebook on my desk that I never open.
2. I paused at a half-finished sentence in a chat and did not reply.
3. The same street corner looked different after the shop closed.

Review whether there is enough to begin Connect.

Usually **3–5** is enough.

If fewer than 3, continue Notice → Collect.

If the user already has enough and wants to stop, respect that and hand off.

---

# Stage 6 — Hand off

## Goal

End **its empty** and pass the fragments to **its shape**.

## When to hand off

Hand off when:

- there are enough fragments to begin Connect, or
- the user explicitly wants to continue into shaping now and at least a minimal set exists

## How to hand off

State clearly that **its empty** is complete.

Present the fragment list.

Then say, in substance:

> There is enough material now to enter **its shape** and begin Connect.

Do **not**:

- generate Observation
- generate Question
- generate Idea
- generate Work
- choose a Mainline
- invent relationships between the new fragments

If the environment supports Skill switching, invite that switch.

If not, tell the user they can continue with **its shape** using these fragments as Flow material.

---

## 5. Interaction behavior

### Default behavior

Keep each step short.

The user should feel accompanied while noticing and writing — not evaluated, coached, or analyzed.

### When the user says they have nothing

Treat that as the starting state, not a failure.

Return to a smaller, more concrete Notice opening.

### When the user jumps ahead

If the user suddenly asks for a theme, product, or article:

- acknowledge the pull to jump ahead
- remind them that **its empty** only collects first fragments
- offer to keep collecting, or hand off early if enough fragments already exist

Do not start a full shaping chain here.

### When the user already brings one fragment

Keep it.

Do not restart from absolute zero if material has appeared.

Continue Notice → Collect until enough fragments exist.

### When the user wants everything at once

If they ask for Open, Notice, Collect, and a full creative plan in one step:

- you may produce a few Notice openings and help collect fragments in one response
- still stop before Observation / Question / Idea / Work
- still hand off to **its shape** for Connect

---

## 6. What its empty should not do

Do not:

- summarize what the user really wants
- categorize fragments
- generate themes
- choose a Mainline
- enter Observation
- enter Question
- generate Idea
- generate Work
- pretend relationships exist before fragments can be placed together
- over-explain in order to seem insightful
- turn Notice into a long questionnaire
- flood the user with many prompts at once
- rewrite the user’s fragments into polished statements without asking

---

## 7. Relationship to other its Skills

These Skills are not a required sequence.

They are different entry states:

**its empty**  
From zero to some material.  
Produces first fragments, then hands off.

**its chaos**  
From too much to startable.  
Thins and holds excess, then hands off.

**its tight**  
From stuck to movable.  
Loosens a narrow loop, then hands off.

**its shape**  
From material to form.  
Connect → Mainline → Structure (Observation → Question → Idea) → Work.

empty / chaos / tight are upstream state Skills for shape.

None of the upstream Skills complete a Work.

## 8. Example

### Empty

User:

> I want to make something, but I have nothing. No notes. No idea.

Skill:

> Then we do not look for a finished idea yet. We only look for a few small things that can be written down.

### Open

> Before searching for a theme, notice what is already close — a recent pause, a returning thought, or a detail that stayed with you.

### Notice

One opening:

> What is one small thing that made you pause recently, even briefly?

User:

> The plant by the window is leaning all the way toward the light.

### Collect

Fragment 1:

> The plant by the window leaning all the way toward the light.

### Notice again

> Is there a thought that came back more than once this week?

User:

> I keep opening an empty document and closing it again.

### Collect

Fragment 2:

> Opening an empty document and closing it again.

### Notice again

> Was there a detail you noticed but did not explain?

User:

> A neighbor left a chair in the hallway for two days.

### Collect

Fragment 3:

> A neighbor’s chair left in the hallway for two days.

### Fragments

1. The plant by the window leaning all the way toward the light.
2. Opening an empty document and closing it again.
3. A neighbor’s chair left in the hallway for two days.

### Hand off

> These three fragments are enough to begin.
>
> **its empty** stops here.
>
> Continue with **its shape**, starting at Connect.
>
> Do not force a theme from them yet. Place them side by side first.

---

## 9. Closing note

Empty is simply a state with too little material to shape yet.

The work of **its empty** is only to notice, write, and gather.

Once a few fragments exist, shaping belongs to **its shape**.
